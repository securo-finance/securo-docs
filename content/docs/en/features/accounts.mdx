Accounts in Securo represent your real-world financial accounts: bank accounts, wallets, credit cards, or any container that holds money. Every transaction belongs to an account, and your total balance is calculated by summing all account balances.

![Accounts](/screenshots/accounts.png)

## Account Types

Securo organizes accounts into two categories:

### Manual Accounts

These are accounts you manage by hand. You create them, set a starting balance, and manually add transactions (or import them from CSV/OFX files). Available types:

- **Checking**
- **Savings**
- **Credit Card**
- **Investment**
- **Wallet**
- **Loan**

A **Loan** account tracks debt: its balance reads as what you still owe, and its settings carry optional **contract terms** (rate, term, first payment, amortization) that turn it into a full loan tracker with a schedule and a payoff date. See [Loans](/docs/features/loans). An account can only switch to or from Loan while it has no transactions.

### Bank Connections

If you connect your bank through the bank sync feature, those accounts appear in a separate section, with the institution's logo next to the connection. Connected accounts can automatically import transactions from your bank, show the account's **masked number** so you can tell similar accounts apart, and can be given a **display name** of your own when the bank's name is unhelpful.

Some banks keep money set aside inside an account, like the piggy banks ("cofrinhos", "caixinhas") of Brazilian banks synced through Pluggy. The account page lists them under **Reserved balances**, each in its own currency, with a subtotal for the ones in the account's currency. Reserved balances are never added to the account balance, net worth or any total, and credit cards don't show them.

Disconnecting a bank keeps these accounts and their history unless you choose to delete them; see [Disconnecting a Bank](/docs/features/bank-sync#disconnecting-a-bank).

### Credit Cards

Credit card accounts carry extra fields that model how a card actually works: **credit limit**, **statement close day**, and **payment due day**. Securo uses them to compute billing cycles and group each purchase into the right statement, so a purchase made after the close date lands on next month's bill, exactly like your card does it.

A card account synced through Pluggy often holds purchases from more than one physical card, like a main card, an additional card and a virtual one. Securo reads the card's last four digits from each purchase and lists the cards it has seen under **Linked cards** in the card's settings, where you can give each one a **label** ("Ana's card", "Virtual"). The transaction list and the transaction dialog then show which card made each purchase, and the **Card** filter on Transactions narrows the list to one or more of them. On a row shared with another member, the owner's card ending stays hidden.

<Callout type="info">
Purchases synced before this feature have no card ending. A self-hosted install can fill them from the data the provider already sent with `backend/scripts/backfill_transaction_card_numbers.py`: run it once without arguments for a dry run that only prints counts, then again with `--apply`, with the Celery worker stopped while it runs.
</Callout>

## Multi-Currency Support

One of Securo's standout features is **native multi-currency support**. Each account has its own currency, and Securo handles conversion automatically.

For example, you might have:
- A checking account in **BRL** (Brazilian Real)
- A savings account in **BRL**
- An investment account in **USD** (US Dollar)

The dashboard and reports will show each account's balance in its native currency, plus the converted equivalent in your primary currency. This makes it easy to see your total net worth across currencies.

When you change your primary currency later, Securo converts the stored values of your transactions, recurring bills and asset purchase prices again in the same step, at the rate of each row's own date, so income, expenses, budgets and reports show numbers in the new currency. A foreign-currency row with no rate for its date is marked as not converted. Account balances don't move, since they are kept in each account's own currency. On a self-hosted install upgraded from a version without this, run it once for data converted before:

```bash
docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml exec backend python -m app.cli restamp-primary-amounts
```

Add a user's email at the end to fix only that user. It is safe to run again.

When no exchange rate is available for an account's currency, Securo shows the balance **in its own currency and says the rate is missing**, rather than passing an unconverted number off as converted, and the totals say when they are incomplete.

## Creating an Account

1. Navigate to **Accounts** in the sidebar
2. Click **+ Add Account**
3. Enter the account name
4. Select the account type (Checking, Savings, Credit Card, etc.)
5. Choose the currency
6. Set the initial balance
7. Click **Save**

The initial balance represents how much is in the account when you start tracking it in Securo. All future transactions will be calculated relative to this starting point.

## Viewing Account Details

Click on any account in the sidebar or the accounts page to see:

- Current balance with percentage change
- Account type and currency
- A filtered list of all transactions for that account

## Closing an Account

An account you no longer use can be **closed** instead of deleted: its history stays intact for reports, but it moves to a separate Closed Accounts section and stops counting toward totals. Closed accounts can be reopened at any time.

## Grouping Accounts with Collections

When you have many accounts, [collections](/docs/features/collections) let you group them (personal, family, a specific project) and filter the dashboard and reports by one group at a time.

## Account Sidebar

Your accounts are always visible in the main sidebar, showing the account name, type, current balance, and a percentage change indicator. This gives you a quick glance at all your balances without navigating away from whatever page you're on. The desktop sidebar can also **collapse to icons**, and it remembers the choice, for anyone who wants the width back.

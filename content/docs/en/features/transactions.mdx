Transactions are the foundation of Securo. Every time money enters or leaves one of your accounts, it's recorded as a transaction. The transactions page provides a powerful interface for viewing, filtering, and managing all your financial activity, as a list or as a cash-flow calendar.

![Transactions](/screenshots/transactions.png)

## Transaction List

The main transactions view shows a sortable table of your activity. Each row can display the description, date, category, account, amount, payee, and more.

- **Checkbox**: Select multiple transactions for bulk actions
- **Description**: The name or memo of the transaction (e.g., "STARBUCKS Latte")
- **Amount**: Positive amounts (green) for income, negative (red) for expenses

Transactions tagged as **transfers** between your own accounts are marked with a special badge so you can tell them apart from regular income and expenses; the badge also **names the linked account**, so a page full of transfers says where the money went without opening each row. Synced transactions also show a **pending** or **posted** status, and a pending row can be **marked as posted in one click**, straight from the row, without opening the edit dialog. Purchases paid in installments display their installment number (e.g., 3/12).

The **description** of a synced transaction is editable too: rename it, and the bank's own text stays underneath with a **Restore original** link. The amount, date and account stay exactly as the bank sent them; editing the manual side of a transfer never changes the amount or date of a synced counterpart either.

### Paid or Unpaid

The pending or posted status says whether the **bank** settled a row. The **paid** mark says whether **you** did: a bill typed in for next week is posted by the bank's clock and still unpaid, and a statement row can be something you still have to settle. Unpaid rows carry an **Unpaid** badge, and one click on the row (or **Mark as paid** / **Mark as unpaid** in the edit dialog) flips it.

- Rows that come from a statement, through bank sync or a file import, start **paid**, since that money already moved. So do group settle-ups and an account's opening balance
- Rows you type, transfers you record and the placeholders a [recurring bill](/docs/features/recurring) creates for a date ahead start **unpaid**. When the bank sync takes over one of them, or a synced or imported charge fulfils a recurring placeholder, it becomes paid
- After that the mark is yours: later syncs never change it
- Transactions that already existed on the update are taken as paid, except those dated in the future, such as installments still to come

The mark doesn't change any total, balance or report yet. Use the **Only unpaid** filter to list what is still to settle; the CSV export follows it too.

### New Transactions to Review

Every transaction that arrives by bank sync or file import starts as **new**, with a **New** badge, until someone opens it. Rows you create in the app, transfers, settle-ups and opening balances start as already read.

The **New only** filter is the review queue. It opens on the whole history, not on the current month, so an import that brought in last month's rows doesn't show "nothing to review"; turning it off puts your date range back. A banner counts the new rows the other filters leave out, and **Mark all as read in this workspace** clears every new row at once, with the count on the button.

### Reporting Date

Sometimes a synced charge lands in the wrong period for your books: the rent debited on the 31st that belongs to next month, a salary paid a day early. Instead of changing the bank's date, set a **Reporting date** in its edit dialog (on a credit card, the effective bill date does this job). Reports, budgets, the dashboard and the transaction list then count the row in the reporting date's period, while the **Bank date** stays as the bank sent it; a moved row shows a badge with its reporting date and keeps its bank date in the list. **Reset reporting date** goes back to the automatic period, and later syncs keep the date you set.

Account running balances still follow the bank date. A posted expense moved to a future period shows in that period's cash-flow outflow without being deducted from the projected balance twice. In the [calendar](#calendar-view), you can switch between the reporting-date and the bank-date view.

### Customizing the Table

The transaction table adapts to how you work:

- **Column picker**: Choose which columns are visible — date, description, category, account, amount, payee, notes, tags, attachments, type, and status
- **Sorting**: Click a column header to sort by it; click again to reverse the direction
- **Resizing**: Drag the edge of a column header to adjust its width

Results are paginated, with navigation controls at the bottom of the list; the page size is selectable and Securo remembers your choice.

### On Mobile

On small screens the table becomes **cards grouped by date** under sticky headers: category icon, description, account, and a color-coded amount, with pending status shown as a badge. Filters, bulk selection, and the List / Calendar switch move into a mobile-friendly menu. The desktop table is unchanged.

## Calendar View

Next to the list, the **Calendar** view puts the month on a grid so you can see where money runs thin before it happens. Each day shows its transactions and projected entries from your [recurring schedules](/docs/features/recurring), and picking a day opens its details in a side panel (or right below the day, on mobile). It's scoped to one account at a time so the running balance means something.

Two independent toggles shape the view, and both choices are remembered:

- **Balance | Activity**: Balance answers "what will I have?", showing each day's running balance with a trend line above the grid. Activity answers "how much moved?", turning the trend into daily bars with income above the axis and expenses below, and making day cells report the real amounts that moved
- **Compact | Detailed**: Compact shows balance and markers only; Detailed lists the top transactions per day with a `+N more`

Projections are never mixed into settled figures: they get their own line, their own dashed bars, and their own block in the day panel. Transfers keep their marker but never count as income or expense.

## Adding a Transaction

There are three ways in: the **+ Add Transaction** button in the top right, a **+** next to **Transactions** in the sidebar from any page, and an **Add Transaction** button on an account page, which already has that account and its currency chosen. In the form the account is **asked for, not preselected**, so nothing lands on the wrong account by accident. Fill in:

1. **Description**: What the transaction is for
2. **Account**: Which account the money came from or went to
3. **Amount**: The transaction value
4. **Date**: When it happened (defaults to today)
5. **Category**: The spending category, or several (see [Splitting Across Categories](#splitting-across-categories))
6. **Payee** (optional): The merchant or person
7. **Status**: **Posted** for money that already moved, **Pending** for a bill you know is coming. Pending transactions are excluded from the dashboard's period totals until they post
8. **Notes** (optional): Any additional context

Transactions also support **file attachments**: you can attach receipts or invoices to any transaction for record keeping. Attached transactions show a paperclip icon in the list. Images, PDFs and XML files are accepted by default (an electronic invoice in XML, for example); images and PDFs open in a preview, and any other file is offered as a download. The server's administrator can change the accepted types (see [Accepted Attachment Types](/docs/deployment/docker#accepted-attachment-types)). The dialog can also **make the transaction recurring** on the spot, creating a schedule from what you just entered.

While filling the form, three helpers speed things up:

- **Description suggestions**: the description field offers descriptions you have used before. Picking one also fills in the category and payee it was last filed under, leaving anything you already chose alone
- **Create on the spot**: when a category or payee search matches nothing, the picker offers to **create it** and selects it in place, in the transaction dialog, the rule dialog and the import review
- **Add or edit the payee in place**: next to the payee field, the transaction dialog has a button that adds a new payee and, once one is picked, another that edits it. Both open the same form as the Payees page (name, type, tax ids, email, phone, website, address and notes), and the payee you save is selected in the transaction. They show only to members who can edit the workspace

Individual transactions can be marked as **ignored**: they stay in the list with a greyed amount and an "Ignored" badge but are left out of totals and reports, useful for reimbursements or corrections you want to keep visible. A gentler option is **Exclude from reports**: the row still counts toward the account balance, it just stops being income or expense in the dashboard and reports, for a work expense put on a personal card or anything else that is not really your spending.

### Installments

A purchase paid in installments can be recorded as one: enter the total, set the **number of installments** (2 to 360) and the **frequency** (weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly), and Securo creates the series with each charge on its date and the `3/12` counter in the list. Editing or deleting one of them asks how far the change should go: **this one**, **this and future**, or **all installments**.

### Duplicating a Transaction

To quickly record a similar transaction, use **Duplicate** on an existing row. Securo copies the description, amount, account, category, and other details into a new transaction for you to adjust. Identity-specific data such as splits is not carried over.

## Transfers

Clicking the **Transfer** button creates a special paired transaction between two of your accounts. For example, moving money from your checking to savings creates:

- A debit in the source account
- A matching credit in the destination account

Transfer transactions are automatically linked and marked with a transfer badge. They don't count as income or expenses in your reports — they're just money moving between your own accounts. When you edit either leg, the dialog shows exactly which transaction sits on the other side (account, date, and amount), and a link takes you **straight to that other side**.

For **cross-currency transfers** (e.g., BRL to USD), Securo asks for both amounts so it can record the correct values in each currency. When you already know what landed on the other side, type the destination amount instead of letting an exchange rate derive it; each leg then carries the exact figure the bank showed.

### Linking and Unlinking Transfers

If a transfer was imported or entered as two separate transactions, select both and **link them as a transfer pair**. Securo then treats them as a single transfer. You can **unlink** a pair at any time to turn them back into independent transactions; unlinking asks for confirmation first, since the freed leg starts counting as income or expense again. [Transfer pairing rules](/docs/features/rules#transfer-pairing-rules) do most of this linking for you: they run on rows from bank sync and file imports, on rows you add by hand, and on a row whose amount, date or account you edit.

## Splitting Across Categories

One purchase often covers more than one kind of spending: a supermarket run with groceries and cleaning supplies, a store receipt with clothes and toiletries. Instead of filing the whole amount under one category, split the transaction so each part lands in its own.

![Splitting a transaction across categories](/screenshots/split-categories.png)

In the add or edit dialog, click **Split across categories** next to the category field, then:

1. Pick a category for each part; **Add category** adds another one. Each category appears once per split
2. Enter the parts by **Amount** or by **%**. Switching between the two converts what you already typed
3. Follow the counter under the parts: it shows how much is **left to allocate**, and **Fill in** puts the rest into the last part. Saving stays blocked until the parts add up to the transaction amount, to the cent (or to exactly 100%)

**Undo split** turns it back into a plain transaction in the category of its largest part.

A split transaction keeps **one row and its full amount**, exactly as the bank sent it; the parts only say how that amount is distributed. In the list it carries a **Split · N** badge where the category would be, and hovering the badge (or tapping it on mobile) shows the parts. Changing the amount of a split transaction asks you to distribute the new amount again.

Everything that totals money by category reads the parts: the dashboard, [reports](/docs/features/reports), [budgets](/docs/features/budgets), the calendar and the list's totals. The **account balance** and the **card bill** keep using the whole amount, since that is what actually left the account. A part in a category marked **Treat as transfer** (part of a deposit going to Investments, say) stays out of income and expenses on its own, while the other parts still count.

[Rules](/docs/features/rules) and **bulk categorize** never overwrite a split: they leave split transactions as they are and say how many they skipped. A purchase in [installments](#installments) can be split too: every installment gets the same split.

Some transactions can't be split across categories: a linked **transfer**, an **ignored** transaction, a transaction **shared with a group**, and a [loan payment](/docs/features/loans) broken into interest and principal. Ignored and hidden categories can't hold a part, and a credit on a credit card can't have a part in a category marked as a transfer.

## Sharing With Other People

When an expense is shared with other people — a dinner, a trip, a shared apartment — you can split it through a **group**. Splitting records each person's share so you can track who owes whom.

You can split a transaction while adding or editing it, or select several transactions and add them to a group in bulk. See the [Groups](/docs/features/groups) guide for the full workflow.

## Flagging for Later

**Flag** a transaction to come back to it: a refund you are waiting for, a charge to dispute, a receipt still to attach. Use the **Flag** button in the transaction dialog, the row's context menu, or the bulk bar for several rows at once.

A flag can carry an optional **reminder** date (shortcuts for tomorrow, in a week or in a month, or a date you pick) and a short **note**. Flagged rows show a marker that turns red once the reminder date has passed, with the date and the note in its tooltip, and the sidebar shows how many flags are overdue. The **Flagged transactions** widget on the [dashboard](/docs/features/dashboard#customizing-the-dashboard) lists them too, overdue ones first.

A flag is only a marker: totals, balances, budgets and reports ignore it. It belongs to the workspace that set it, so a transaction shared from another workspace shows neither the flag nor its note.

## Filtering and Search

The filter bar at the top provides multiple ways to narrow down your transactions:

- **Search**: Free-text search across descriptions and notes
- **Account**: Filter by a specific account
- **Category**: Filter by one or more categories, or show only **uncategorized** transactions. A split transaction shows up when any of its parts is in the filtered categories, and the totals at the bottom count only those parts
- **Payee**: Filter by merchant or person
- **Type**: Show only income, only expenses, or **transfers** (both linked pairs and transactions in a category marked as a transfer, including split transactions with a part in one; the totals then count only the transfer parts)
- **Status**: All, posted only, or pending only
- **Only unpaid**: rows still [marked as unpaid](#paid-or-unpaid)
- **New only**: the [review queue](#new-transactions-to-review) of rows nobody opened yet
- **Hide ignored**: drop ignored rows from the list
- **Amount**: Set a minimum and/or maximum value
- **Date range**: Set start and end dates with the date picker
- **Tags**: Filter by hashtags used in transaction notes
- **Group**: Show transactions split through a specific group
- **Flagged**: All flagged, **Overdue** or **Due this week**. These views open on the whole history instead of the current month

All filters can be combined to create precise queries like "all uncategorized expenses from my credit card this month." Active filters are saved in the page URL, so you can reload, bookmark, or share a filtered view.

## Export

Click **Export CSV** to download your transactions as a CSV file. The export respects your current filters and selection, so you can export a specific subset of transactions.

Text cells that start with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return (a description, payee, account, category or note) get a leading apostrophe, so a spreadsheet opens them as text instead of running them as a formula. This matters because bank sync and imports bring in text written by someone else. Amounts and dates are exported as plain values, so a negative amount stays a number.

A transaction split across categories is exported as **one row per part**, tied together by the `split_group` and `split_index` columns, so the file adds up by category and can be [imported back](/docs/features/import#split-transactions-in-a-csv) as the same split. The full workspace backup keeps the parts of split transactions and of split recurring bills as well.

### For Your Accountant

A **Business** workspace also has an **Accountant export** card in the workspace settings. Pick a start and end date (it starts on the first day of the current month and ends today) and click **Download package** to get one zip with two files:

- `transactions.csv`: one row per category part, with split shares and both legs of a transfer included
- `profit-and-loss.csv`: income and expenses per category and the result of the period, in the workspace's primary currency, with transfers and ignored categories left out

Both files use semicolons, decimal commas, `DD/MM/YYYY` dates and UTF-8 with a BOM, so spreadsheets and accounting tools open them without adjustment. Amounts are the full value of each transaction, before splits with other people, so the result can differ from the in-app income and expense report. A viewer can download it too, and the period goes back at most ten years.

## Bulk Actions

Select multiple transactions using the checkboxes to reveal the bulk action bar. From there you can:

- **Categorize** all selected transactions at once. Transactions split across categories keep their parts, and Securo tells you how many it left as they were
- **Add tags** to the selection
- **Add to a group** with a split rule
- **Link** two transactions as a transfer pair
- **Flag** or **unflag** the selection
- **Delete** multiple transactions at once, behind a confirmation that names what is about to go. Transfer counterparts and attachments are cleaned up with them

Right-clicking the selection opens a **context menu** with the same actions, closer to the rows you are working on.

## Questions From Payment Matching

A transaction that may settle an invoice or a recurring bill, but that the [matching rules](/docs/features/reconciliation) could not bind on their own, carries a **Question** badge. Open the queue under Rules to answer it; a transaction that was matched shows the invoice it settled instead.
